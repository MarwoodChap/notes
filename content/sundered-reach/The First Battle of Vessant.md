# The First Battle of Vessant

**Date:** 28.10.2026 **Combatants:** Emperor's Children (The Discordant Duet) vs Aeldari (Craftworld Iyanden, with Harlequin allies) **World:** Vessant **Result:** Emperor's Children victory, 38–20

---

## First account — The Discordant Duet

> _"Their weapons know one note, and they play it until the listener dies of boredom. Mine sing."_ — Lord Kakophonist Malakor

Kaelen and Sylas of the Discordant Duet had heard whispers of a relic hidden on the increasingly neglected shrine world of Vessant. A relic that could act as a tuning fork for their grand plans for the ultimate sonic performance within the Sundered Reach. They travelled to the largely abandoned world with a warband of their forces, intending to search for the relic and secure it. Of course, most of their warband enjoyed themselves in the way only Slaanesh worshippers can as they hunted across the world. They eventually narrowed their search to an area of ruined churches.

Farseer Aethenor has seen many things, predicting various horrors that have come to pass for his race, but also a vision of a future where Iyanden returns to its former glories. His latest premonitions pointed him towards a mon-keigh religious backwater, where visions of Slaanesh worshippers growing in strength and influencing the future of his Craftworld worried him greatly.

Aethenor and his army arrived at the same place at the same time as the Discordant Duet. Immediately, both forces recognised the importance of this clash: the race that had, to their eternal shame, given birth to a new Chaos god, and the worshippers of that god. Aethenor desired to find out more about this previously unknown warband — surveillance was the key for him. Kaelen and Sylas immediately recognised the importance of diverting attention away from their relic-hunting purpose — setting decoys to prevent this surveillance became their priority. Both forces were keenly aware of the importance of dealing with any large war vehicles and machines, and of how surgical strikes to neutralise key leaders in the opposing army would be critical.

The scene was set for this incursion battle. Chaos Rhinos were swiftly deployed and pushed forward by Kaelen and Sylas, whilst Sylas's Infractors dashed forward to the suspected location of the relic. Aethenor held back a large part of his force, but deployed a mysterious Wave Serpent to draw the attention of the Chaos worshippers.

Early skirmishes saw Aeldari Dark Reapers, stationed in a hidden position above the main battlefield, cause significant damage. Fearing their deadly weapons, and bored by the monotonous sound they made, Kaelen ordered Malakor and his Noise Marines, hidden inside a Chaos Rhino, to show these dangerous foes how a more creative sonic approach is superior. This approach worked better than Kaelen could have imagined. Dashing out of their transport, the Noise Marines' sonic weapons cut through the cover the Reapers thought they had, and the head-splitting sound waves turned the valorous Aeldari snipers inside out. Malakor even skilfully ushered his marines back into their transport to avoid the worst of the return fire no doubt coming their way.

On the other flank of the battlefield, Aethenor's Wave Serpent made short work of the armoured transport opposing it, forcing a desperate escape from the burning vehicle by Kaelen's elite Flawless Blades. Under severe fire from surrounding units, the Blades' desperate attempts to find cover were only partially successful. Knowing that another round of fire from the Wave Serpent would be terminal, the elite swordsmen decided to engage the Aeldari battle vehicle in close combat, calling on their daemonic sponsors to quicken their blades. These eldritch beings gladly offered their support, allowing the Blades to significantly damage, but not destroy, the Serpent. As a result of this failure, one swordsman was dragged screaming (in ecstasy?) into the warp by his daemonic sponsor, to unknown ends.

A unit of Harlequins and a Solitaire, proving themselves more useful and more faithful to Aethenor than his premonitions about them had suggested, ultimately saw off the remaining Flawless Blades, the Solitaire dealing the final blow — his lightning reflexes allowing him to strike before even the last of the elite Blades could react. At the same point in the battle the Wave Serpent, which had made a desperate retreat from close combat, was destroyed by Kaelen's own unit of Tormentors.

As the middle of the battle approached, Aethenor summoned his deep strike and reserve units to try to turn the tide and sift through the decoys the Slaanesh worshippers had set for the Aeldari. This met with mixed success. Specialist anti-tank Fire Dragons annihilated the remaining Chaos Rhino, but quickly faced sonic destruction from Malakor and his marines emerging from the wreckage. Malakor's battle high continued to the end of the battle, his Noise Marines demonstrating the agonising beauty of discordant sound to many other Aeldari troops. Other Aeldari reinforcements whittled away at Kaelen's units, but ultimately could not dislodge the Chaos fanatics from the key points of the battlefield.

As the battle neared its end, two events defined it.

Firstly, it became apparent that Sylas's Infractors had played minimal part in the battle. A dark rumour spread amongst the survivors that dataslates featuring daemonette images of illicit and unknown provenance had been engaging the normally brutal infantry unit. Another whisper was that the Infractors' usual excess of combat drugs had led to a shared, drug-induced hallucination. The more mundane truth was that the unit had chanced upon the relic the warband sought, and were ensuring it was protected throughout the battle. Even this simple task proved too much for some of the savages in the unit, who emerged towards the end of the battle to take pot shots at Aeldari units in the centre of the battlefield.

Secondly, and despite the overall success of the battle for the Emperor's Children, precision shots from the Aeldari had caused Kaelen to fall. His death could not be confirmed, as no body was found in the aftermath. His Tormentors carried his signature power spear off the battlefield, certain that Kaelen still lived, and telling tales of Kaelen's joyous voice as he fell:

> _"A wound so perfect, it borders on art. You have given me a taste of the ultimate threshold… I need this sensation again and again… this pain will be the first note of my grandest symphony on my return."_

## Second account — The Iyanden telling

> _"I came to see them. I did. They are worse than I saw."_ — Farseer Aethenor

_Draft — for Richard to take, alter or discard._

Aethenor came to Vessant to watch, not to win. A shrine world the mon-keigh no longer troubled to pray at, and in its ruins the children of She Who Thirsts growing strong enough to matter. He wished to know their names. That was all.

The followers of the Prince had prepared for watchers. Every glance Iyanden took across the ruined churches returned something meant for it: a hollow position, a vehicle that drew fire and carried no one of consequence, a movement that went nowhere. The Farseer read the field as he reads all fields, and for the first time in a long while found the field reading him back.

The painted ones did on the left what Aethenor had foreseen they would not, and he records it without complaint. He has been wrong before. He finds he does not mind being wrong in that direction.

Iyanden lost the field. It did not lose the look. One of the twins fell to an Aeldari shot and was carried away still speaking, and that is the thing Aethenor cannot set down: not the wound, but that it was received as a gift.

At the edge of the ruins, one unit of the Prince's most savage did not fight for most of the day. That is not in their nature. Something there was worth their not fighting. Aethenor did not see what.

## Consequences

The Discordant Duet hold the ruined churches of Vessant, and hold **Component II**, the relic their Infractors found and guarded through the battle. They intend to strike it.

They were not the first to find it. Beneath the ruined churches the ground had been opened, and closed again, before either force arrived. Whoever did so reached the component first, had every opportunity to take it, and left it where it lay. At Cerelia and at Auspicia they took what mattered. At Vessant they took nothing.

Lord Exultant Kaelen fell to Aeldari fire. No body was recovered. His Tormentors carried his power spear from the field and insist he lives. Kaelen, who has spent ten thousand years despising his brother's appetites, has found one of his own.

Craftworld Iyanden withdrew having achieved what Aethenor came for: the warband is named, counted and watched. It did not see what the Duet took, and does not know that it should have.

Vessant has been fought over once.
