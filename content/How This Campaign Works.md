# How This Campaign Works

*Draft — needs agreeing with the group before first battle. Everything marked **TBC** is a genuine open question, not a decision already made.*

---

## Cadence

Roughly **one game a month**, sometimes less. That shapes everything below.

Practical consequences:
- **Nobody will remember what happened.** The [[./Battle Log|Battle Log]] isn't optional flavour, it's the campaign's memory. A game that isn't written up didn't happen.
- **No structure that needs all four players present.** Any two people who can get a table should be able to play a campaign game.
- **Arcs of three games**, so a chapter completes in a season rather than a decade.
- **Book dates a month ahead.** With this cadence, "we'll sort something out" means four months pass.

## Points and rules — TBC

| | Proposal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Points** | 1,000 to start, rising to 2,000 | Small enough that everyone can field a painted force early |
| **Ruleset** | 11th edition | Narrative rules in 11th are still bedding in — worth checking what the *Dominatus* campaign deck actually does before committing to it |
| **Progression** | Light | Full Crusade tracking is a lot of admin for monthly play. Suggest: units earn a name and one trait, and that's it |
| **Codex churn** | Whatever's current on the app | 11th is new and codexes are reissuing. Nobody re-buys books mid-campaign |

## Painting — TBC, and this is the one that kills campaigns

Suggested: **models should be painted to a basic tabletop standard to earn campaign progression**, but unpainted models can still play. Carrot, not stick.

Worth agreeing explicitly and up front, because the alternative is one person quietly resenting three others for eight months.

## Counts-as

Assume yes, generously, for everyone. Crosius fields Sisters, Mechanicus and Arbites as Astra Militarum datasheets; expect the same latitude to be extended in return.

## Balance

This is not a tournament. If it's clear at the bottom of turn one that a game is a blowout, re-rack — change the terrain, the mission, or the list, and play the game that's worth playing.

## What happens on the table is canon

Win or lose. If Crosius loses Cerelia, Cerelia is lost, and it stays lost until somebody takes it back. The fiction follows the dice, not the other way round.

**The exception:** nobody's character dies without their agreement. Everything else is fair game.

---

## The four-way problem

Three Chaos-and-xenos forces against one Imperial one is a 3v1 unless we build against it. We're not doing that.

**The relics are the shared objective.** Every faction wants them or wants them denied to someone else, and no two factions want the same outcome. That makes this a four-cornered scramble rather than an alliance against James.

Specifically worth leaning on:
- **World Eaters and Emperor's Children are not friends.** Khorne and Slaanesh, and two Legions with a long and specific history. There is no reason for Tristan and Michael to be on the same side of any table.
- **The Aeldari have their own reason to care what Slaanesh gets**, which is the oldest reason in the setting.
- **Everyone is short of everything.** Isolation is a shared condition, not an Imperial problem.

## Tristan's spare Chaos Marines

Best use: a **floating NPC force**. Renegades, the human pirates preying on Crosius shipping, or whoever the mission needs. Run by whoever isn't playing that week, or by both players in a co-op mission. It also means a three-player evening has a fourth force available.

## Before first battle — checklist

- [ ] Everyone writes their [[./House Crosius|faction page]] — leader, motive, why they're in the Reach, what they want the relics for
- [ ] Agree points level and progression weight
- [ ] Agree the painting expectation, out loud
- [ ] Agree what happens to Tristan's spare army
- [ ] Decide whether the Reach is where everyone *was*, or where everyone *arrived*
- [ ] Pick the first flashpoint — one world, two factions, a reason
- [ ] Book the first two dates

## Writing up

After each game, whoever won writes three or four sentences. Not a battle report — a story beat. What was fought over, what was lost, what changed. It goes in [[./Battle Log|Battle Log]] and the relevant world page gets updated.

Three sentences a month is sustainable. A thousand-word report is not, and the campaign that demands one dies in March.
