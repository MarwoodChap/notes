# Rules Summary

A player's crib sheet for *Arkham Horror – The Roleplaying Game*, condensed from the rules in the Free RPG Day 2026 adventure *Three Days to the Big Easy*. Anything specific to your investigator (knacks, spells, kit, personality trait) is on your character sheet.

> [!info] How we play
> Teams, 8–10:30pm. Roll your own dice and report the results. You need **six standard d6s** plus **a few d6s in a different colour** to use as horror or music dice.

## The dice pool

- Your pool holds up to **six d6s**. It is both your **health** and your **effort**: how much you can do in a scene, or in a single turn of a fight.
- Damage lowers your **dice pool limit** (the number of dice your pool refills to). Your **dice pool maximum** is the limit when you're unhurt, normally 6.
- Dice you spend are set aside until the pool refills. Pools refill:
  - at the start of each session
  - at the start of each new scene
  - at the start of the investigators' turn in a fight
- Everyone's pool is visible, including allies' and enemies'. Use that when deciding whether to hold dice back.
- Some knacks and kit give **bonus dice** for a single roll. They're used once and then discarded; they never raise your limit.

## Rolling

- Every skill has a target number from **2+ (best)** to **6+ (worst)**. This is set by the relevant skill on your character sheet, so lower is better.
- Decide how many dice from your pool to commit, then roll them. **Each die at or above your skill is a success.** One success is enough for most actions. In combat, it makes sense to hold some dice back for defensive **Reactions**.
- **Difficult** actions need **two successes**; **very difficult** need **three**.
- **Advantage:** roll one extra die, then remove the lowest. **Disadvantage:** roll one extra die, then remove the highest. You can have both at once.
- Order of resolution: roll → rerolls → remove dice (advantage/disadvantage) → effects triggered by particular results → modifiers → count successes.
- You can't retry the same action in the same scene (or the same turn in a fight) unless you change something, such as a new approach or a new circumstance.

**Advantage and disadvantage** are how the game handles circumstances. Advantage covers anything that helps: a friend aiding you, good preparation, or knowing exactly where to look. Disadvantage covers anything that gets in the way: bad light, a rushed attempt, or trying to search while someone is shooting at you. Both work the same way mechanically. You add **one extra die** to your roll. With advantage you then remove the **lowest** result, and with disadvantage you remove the **highest**. You can have both at once, in which case you add two dice and remove one of each. The GM decides when they apply, but it’s always worth making the case: describe how you’re stacking the odds.

In practice, advantage is roughly a free extra die. It only costs you a success if every die you rolled succeeded. Disadvantage is much harsher, because it always strips out your best result. If you roll a single die with disadvantage, you only succeed if **both** dice hit. When you’re at a disadvantage, commit more dice than you normally would. Advantage can come from a friend spending a die to aid you, from spending a point of insight, or from the situation. Insight can’t cancel disadvantage, though, so the better answer is often to change the circumstances first.

## Types of action

### Simple actions

Things that are unlikely to fail: moving about 10 feet, standing up or lying down, opening a door, grabbing or stowing an item, pulling a lever, reloading when not under pressure, or **aiding an ally** (gives them advantage on their next complex action).

- **Outside a fight:** free and automatic.
- **In a fight:** cost one die each, and succeed automatically.

### Complex actions

Anything with a real chance of failure. Describe what you're trying to do, the GM picks the skill, and you roll. Common ones:

| **Action**                | **Skill**                              | **Notes**                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Attack                | Melee / Ranged / Lore              | See *Attacks and damage*                                                          |
| Disengage             | Melee Combat                       | Move up to 10 ft away from enemies you're engaged with                            |
| Reload under pressure | Agility                            | Only needed in a dangerous situation                                              |
| Heal wounds           | Knowledge                          | Yourself or someone adjacent; +1 dice pool limit per success; outside fights only |
| Heal horror           | Resolve (self) / Presence (others) | Reduces horror die limit by 1; takes hours; outside fights only                   |
| Cast a spell          | As per the spell                   | See your character sheet                                                          |

### Reactions

Done in response to something, usually on the other side's turn.

- Always **one die only**, and **one reaction per action** against you.
- **Dodge** a ranged attack (Agility), **block** a melee attack (Melee Combat), or **resist** a magical attack. Success means you avoid the damage and every other effect.

## Narrative scenes

Most of the game. Investigating, talking, travelling.

- Spend dice on complex actions as you see fit; simple actions are free.
- When the group has spent its dice and still wants to do more, the scene ends and time moves on, usually 15 minutes to an hour.
- If you run out before everyone else, you can still talk and plan, but your investigator is busy until the next scene.

## Structured scenes (fights)

Played in **rounds**. Each round is one **investigators' turn** and one **GM turn**. Whoever started the trouble goes first; if you're ambushed, the GM goes first.

**Investigators' turn**
1. Refill your pool to your current limit.
2. Act in any order you agree among yourselves, splitting your actions however you like. If you can't agree, the GM decides.
3. The turn ends when everyone has spent their dice or **passed**.

**Passing:** stop spending on your turn and keep the remaining dice for reactions (dodging and blocking) during the GM's turn. Once you pass, you can't spend more dice on your own turn.

**Allies:** friendly NPCs act on the investigators' turn; the GM runs them.

### Attacks and damage

- **Melee:** target within 5 feet. Characters within 5 feet of each other are **engaged**.
- **Ranged:** target more than 5 feet away; weapon range is on the item.
- **Magic:** usually Lore.
- A successful hit deals the weapon's damage, usually 1 or 2. Some armour or knacks reduce it.
- Damage lowers your **dice pool limit**. If you're now holding more dice than your new limit, discard the extras. The limit stays down until healed.
- Ranges are in 5-foot steps; one map square is 5 ft × 5 ft.

### Strain yourself

At the start of the investigators' turn, before refilling, you can **strain yourself**: restore your limit to maximum. At the end of that turn you suffer an **injury**. Outside a fight, the injury comes after your first complex action.

### Injuries

Some hits, and straining yourself, cause an injury. Roll a d6 (+1 for each injury you already have) and the GM reads the result from the Injury table. Results run from being knocked prone, through wounded limbs and lost senses, to coma and death at the top end.

- Heal naturally in a week (two weeks for the worst ones), or with a Knowledge complex action outside a fight. Some injuries need several successes.
- Repeat injuries don't stack, but each one has to be healed separately.

## Horror

Horror is mental damage.

- When you suffer horror, your **horror die limit** goes up by that amount (never above your dice pool maximum).
- When your pool refills, put in **horror dice first** (one per point of horror die limit), then fill the rest with normal dice. If your horror limit is as high as your pool limit, your whole pool is horror dice.
- Horror dice roll like normal dice, with two differences:
  1. You can **never reroll a 1** on a horror die.
  2. If **any horror die rolls a 1** during an action, you suffer a **trauma**.
- When you take damage, remove normal dice before horror dice.

### Trauma

Roll a d6 and add 1 for each 1 rolled on horror dice in that action. Low results are an unsettling moment with no lasting effect. Higher results make you lose dice from your pool, trigger the negative side of your personality trait (which you may be able to cancel with insight), and at the very top you are **lost forever**. Some results make later trauma rolls worse for the rest of the session.

Traumas can't be healed, but they fade with time.

### Healing horror

The *Heal Horror* action or certain knacks reduce your horror die limit. That only affects the next refill; horror dice already in your pool stay put.

## Insight

You start each session with insight up to the maximum on your sheet. Spend 1 insight to:

- add **one success** to a complex action that already succeeded
- give yourself **advantage** before rolling a complex action or reaction
- introduce a **narrative element**, such as finding a useful object or having an existing connection with an NPC (with the GM's approval; bigger asks may cost more)
- avoid the effects of certain traumas, where stated
- use the **positive side of your personality trait**

## Personality traits

Every investigator has one, with a positive and a negative side. The positive side costs insight. The negative side is triggered by trauma or by events in the story, and lasts as described on your sheet.

## Quick reference

| **I want to…**            | **Do this**                                                         |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do something risky    | Complex action: commit dice, any die ≥ skill succeeds           |

| Keep dice for defence | Pass on your turn                                               |
| Get back in the fight | Strain yourself, but take an injury                             |
