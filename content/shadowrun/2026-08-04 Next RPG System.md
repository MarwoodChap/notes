# Next RPG System — Candidate Overview

We've wrapped our **Shadowrun SR6** run. I enjoyed it; you didn't — most likely the rules weight, as SR6 is one of the heaviest mainstream systems going. We're voting on the next system. These are notes on each candidate.

> [!tip] The axis that matters: The biggest difference between these options is **rules weight (crunch)**, because that's the thing that split the table. Each entry flags it, and there's a summary table at the bottom.

## The candidates

### D20 far-future sci-fi (name TBC)

Almost certainly one of two things — worth pinning down **before** we vote, as they play very differently:

- **Traveller20 (T20)** — an early-2000s d20 conversion of classic _Traveller_. Fairly crunchy; class-and-level bolted onto the _Traveller_ setting, complete with its famously deadly character _creation_ (your character can die before play even begins).
- **d20 Future** — the sci-fi companion to d20 Modern. Mechanically it _is_ d20 Modern plus starships, robots and gear.

If whoever suggested it also put D20 Modern on the list, **d20 Future** is the likely answer and the two options effectively merge. → **Action: confirm which one this is.**

### D20 Modern

WotC's early-2000s d20 system for contemporary / near-future settings. Ability-score-driven classes plus an "action points" mechanic. **Medium crunch** — lighter than SR6, heavier than anything narrative. Out of print and dated (D&D 3.5-era tech), but instantly familiar to anyone who's played 3.x D&D. A genre-agnostic toolkit rather than a setting.

There is also **Everyday Heroes**, which is 5E, but in a modern cinematic, action movie genre.

### Carbon 2185

A cyberpunk setting built **on D&D 5e**. The shrewd pick if we liked Shadowrun's _world_ but not its _rules_: same neon-noir megacorp / cyberware / hacking vibe, running on 5e so anyone who's touched modern D&D can play at once. Reviews praise the atmosphere and accessibility, with the usual caveat that 5e's fantasy chassis shows seams under cyberpunk (hacking and cyberware are grafted on). **Low-to-medium crunch.** The closest tonal replacement for what we enjoyed.

### Shadowrun Anarchy

The **rules-light, narrative** version of Shadowrun, using the "Cue System": shared storytelling, plot points, players narrating their own scenes, minimal dice-fishing. Same beloved setting, a fraction of SR6's mechanical load. The obvious compromise candidate — keeps the world while directly addressing the complaint. Divisive, though: fans of Shadowrun's tactical depth find it _too_ loose and under-supported, and it assumes some setting familiarity. **Low crunch.**

### Shadowrun SR6

The incumbent we rejected. On the list only as a baseline for comparison. **Heavy crunch.**

### Warhammer 40,000 RPG

Important caveat: "the 40k RPG" isn't one game, it's several, and **which one matters enormously**.

- **Fantasy Flight line** (Dark Heresy, Rogue Trader, Deathwatch, Black Crusade, Only War) — d100, gritty, lethal, **crunchy** (SR6-level or worse). Probably the wrong direction for us.
- **Wrath & Glory** (Cubicle 7) — d6 dice-pool, more heroic, **medium crunch**.
- **Imperium Maledictum** (Cubicle 7) — d100 but streamlined, investigation/intrigue focused.

Grimdark far-future tone, very strong setting. **Action: pin down which game** before voting. Note most 40k RPG play is as fragile humans in a hostile Imperium, not Space Marines (that's Deathwatch / Wrath & Glory).

### Call of Cthulhu

The safe, evergreen choice. Chaosium's Basic Roleplaying (d100 percentile): **low-to-medium crunch**, very easy to teach, mechanics that genuinely serve the fiction (investigation, sanity erosion, ordinary people out of their depth). 1920s horror by default but adaptable to any era. The tonal opposite of Shadowrun — slower, investigative, deadly-but-not-tactical. A near-guaranteed good time.

### Rivers of London

Chaosium's newest of these, based on Ben Aaronovitch's urban-fantasy novels (a Met officer who becomes a wizard; magical policing in modern London). Runs on the **same Basic Roleplaying engine as Call of Cthulhu**, but deliberately lighter and gentler. Reviews praise the atmosphere and GM guidance but land on a consistent criticism: it's **too forgiving** — failure is nearly impossible and death very unlikely, so stakes and tension suffer. **Low crunch**, cosy-mystery tone. Great for low-lethality investigative fun in a contemporary British setting; weak if we want jeopardy.

## My recommendation

Given the split at the table, the three most likely to satisfy both camps:

1. **Carbon 2185** — Shadowrun's world, far lighter rules.
2. **Shadowrun Anarchy** — literally Shadowrun made light.
3. **Call of Cthulhu** — a clean break that reliably runs well.
