# Blues Clues

What the investigators have found so far in the case of Leon Marsh, and what it seems to suggest. Theories are the investigators' own; nothing here is confirmed.

## What happened

- Leon Marsh vanished mid-set at the Mysterioso, inside a blue flame. Witnesses felt no heat, nothing burned, and the flame made no sound. Every flame in the room leaned towards the stage just before it happened.
- Leon seemed to be expecting something. Between numbers he kept looking at the stair door.
- His trumpet fell to the stage. Kieran Doyle, the second trumpet, picked it up in the confusion and ran. PC O'Neil chased him but lost him in the crowd.

## What was left behind

- **A silver crown** on Leon's chair, dated 1937: George VI's coronation year. It's cold, though everything else on the stage was warm.
- **A silver dollar on a chain**, dated 1921, lying on the boards with its clasp still fastened, as if it had fallen from a neck that wasn't there any more. It stays warm.
- **The set list**, taped to the floor by Leon's chair. The last number, *The Old Man's Tune*, is crossed out so hard the pen went through the paper. In the margin: NOT ANY MORE.

## The tune

- Leon struck *The Old Man's Tune* off his set list that night.
- Kieran was found in his bedsit in Kentish Town playing a fragment of a tune over and over, getting a little further each time. He said the trumpet wanted to be played, and wanted the tune to be "finished".

## The trumpet

- Old, American, repaired many times, with a dent in the rim of the bell.
- Names are scratched round the bell in many different hands. The newest is **Leon Marsh**, still bright. Kieran swears it wasn't there before that night. The names get more worn the older they are, and the oldest are worn smooth.
- Among the readable names: Tommy Wierzbicki, Grace Achterberg, Delroy Fenwick, Ray Callis, and the oldest that can still be read, **Eddie S.**
- Its magical traces are overwhelming: the tune, warm brass, a firm handshake, a coin rotating on a wooden table, and dust from a country road in summer. To the investigators, they suggest a crossroads.
- The trumpet, the crown and the dollar are now with Nightingale. Nobody has touched the trumpet with bare hands since it was recovered.

## The photograph

- Found tucked inside the jazz book from the bookshop. It shows a five-piece band at **the Blue Lantern, Gerrard Street**, in Coronation week, May 1937. The trumpeter at the front has a horn with the **same dent** in its bell. The same trumpet has probably been in London since 1937.
- On the back: *Eddie S. and the boys.* Underneath, in a different hand: *Horn's paid for. Not by me.*

## Lord Grant

- He recognised **Leon**, who he'd heard play; **Delroy Fenwick**, a London trumpeter he knew; and **Eddie S.**, but only as an old story told by the musicians who taught him.
- He dismissed **Ray Callis** as "a rock guitarist. Not my world."

## Rupert Ashdown

- He said he was "with the Cabinet Office, in a manner of speaking". He wanted to know where the trumpet was and didn't ask about Leon.
- He told them to find it "before anyone plays it all the way through".
- His card has a mobile number and nothing else. On the back, in fountain pen: *With compliments. — C.*

## The pattern so far

Musicians who are ordinary for years suddenly become very good. Then they disappear.

## Open questions

- Where is Leon?
- Who, or what, is *The Old Man's Tune* for?
- Who was Eddie S., and who paid for the horn?
- What happened to Delroy Fenwick?
- Who is "C."?
