# Blues Clues

What the investigators have found so far in the case of Leon Marsh, and what it seems to suggest. Theories are the investigators' own; nothing here is confirmed.

## What happened

- Leon Marsh vanished mid-set at the Mysterioso, inside a blue flame. Witnesses felt no heat, nothing burned, and the flame made no sound. Every flame in the room leaned towards the stage just before it happened.
- Leon seemed to be expecting something. Between numbers he kept looking at the stair door.
- His trumpet fell to the stage. Kieran Doyle, the second trumpet, picked it up in the confusion and ran. PC O'Neil chased him but lost him in the crowd.

## What was left behind

- **A silver crown** on Leon's chair, dated 1937: George VI's coronation year. It's cold, though everything else on the stage was warm.
- **A silver dollar on a chain**, dated 1921, lying on the boards with its clasp still fastened, as if it had fallen from a neck that wasn't there any more. It stays warm.
- **The set list**, taped to the floor by Leon's chair. The last number, *The Old Man's Tune*, is crossed out so hard the pen went through the paper. In the margin: NOT ANY MORE.

## The tune

- Leon struck *The Old Man's Tune* off his set list that night.
- Kieran was found in his bedsit in Kentish Town playing a fragment of a tune over and over, getting a little further each time. He said the trumpet wanted to be played, and wanted the tune to be "finished".
- **Kieran is now playing out.** Dee Halloran rang PC O'Neil: Kieran is playing an open mic, and he's suspiciously good. She texted a photo of his set list. The last entry, added in a different pen, is *The Old Man's Tune ?*, with the note *no name — just play it*. The trumpet is still locked up at the Folly.

## The trumpet

- Old, American, repaired many times, with a dent in the rim of the bell.
- Names are scratched round the bell in many different hands. The newest is **Leon Marsh**, still bright. Kieran swears it wasn't there before that night. The names get more worn the older they are, and the oldest are worn smooth.
- Among the readable names: Tommy Wierzbicki, Grace Achterberg, Delroy Fenwick, Ray Callis, and the oldest that can still be read, **Eddie S.**
- Its magical traces are overwhelming: the tune, warm brass, a firm handshake, a coin rotating on a wooden table, and dust from a country road in summer. To the investigators, they suggest a crossroads.
- The trumpet, the crown and the dollar are now with Nightingale. Nobody has touched the trumpet with bare hands since it was recovered.

## The photograph

- Found tucked inside the jazz book from the bookshop. It shows a five-piece band at **the Blue Lantern, Gerrard Street**, in Coronation week, May 1937. The trumpeter at the front has a horn with the **same dent** in its bell. The same trumpet has probably been in London since 1937.
- On the back: *Eddie S. and the boys.* Underneath, in a different hand: *Horn's paid for. Not by me.*

## Leon's flat

- **The burglar.** A local addict was searching the flat when the investigators arrived. They caught him. He'd been paid £50 to look for a trumpet, and hadn't found it. He walks with a limp.
- **Leon's notebook.** The burglar dropped it as he fled. It's now with the investigators (see below).
- **The gas bill** (found by PC O'Neil): paid in full, early, by someone else. Leon seems to have been putting his affairs in order.
- **The set lists** (found by Dr Carmichael): a stack of Leon's old set lists, every gig closing with the same untitled number. Three weeks ago it stops, and his handwriting gets worse from then on.

#### Leon's notebook

A black notebook, swollen from life in a gig bag. The front half is ordinary: chord charts, set ideas, phone numbers. In the back half the handwriting changes, starting neat and getting cramped, then frantic. Some pages have been torn out.

**Leon's research.** Photocopied pages glued in about the crossroads legend. Robert Johnson has a question mark beside him, and underneath Leon has written *"Wrong Johnson. Tommy said it first."* There are notes on Papa Legba and on crossroads figures from other traditions, each marked with a tick or a cross, as if Leon were trying to work out exactly who he'd met. One line is underlined twice: *"They never cheat you. They just read the small print back to you."*

**Leon's own entries, in order:**
1. *"Played it once in the empty room after closing. He was at the bar. Never saw him come in."*
2. *"Asked what it cost. He said he'd let me know the terms. Terms. Like a loan."*
3. *"It's a price, not a gift. He was very clear on that. Polite about it."*
4. *"Best year of my life."*
5. About three weeks ago: *"He came back. Said it would be collected. Not when, just that it would be."*
6. *"Stopped playing it. It isn't mine to give away any more."*
7. The last entry: *"K keeps humming it. He's been learning it off me without me knowing. I never taught him. DON'T let him play it."*

Leon never describes the man's face. Every entry says only "he".

## Maud and Delroy Fenwick

- Maud, the elderly regular from the Mysterioso, is **Delroy Fenwick's twin sister** (older by twenty minutes). She told the investigators that Delroy disappeared in the **1970s**.
- Delroy had an arrangement with someone he called **"the Gentleman"**, and said **the bill always comes due**.
- When Delroy vanished, a **silver crown** was left behind, just as with Leon.

## Nightingale's view

- He examined the coins. The **warm** one comes from a deal that is still **open**; the **cold** ones from deals that are **closed**.
- He described Rupert Ashdown as some sort of Cabinet Office flunky, and a coward.

## Lord Grant

- He recognised **Leon**, who he'd heard play; **Delroy Fenwick**, a London trumpeter he knew; and **Eddie S.**, but only as an old story told by the musicians who taught him.
- He dismissed **Ray Callis** as "a rock guitarist. Not my world."

## Rupert Ashdown

- He said he was "with the Cabinet Office, in a manner of speaking". He wanted to know where the trumpet was and didn't ask about Leon.
- He told them to find it "before anyone plays it all the way through".
- His card has a mobile number and nothing else. On the back, in fountain pen: *With compliments. — C.*
- The investigators have arranged to meet him at **11:00 tomorrow, on the benches in St James's Park near Great George Street**.

## The pattern so far

Musicians who are ordinary for years suddenly become very good. They seem to make an arrangement with someone, and the bill comes due. Then they disappear, and a silver crown is left behind.

## The investigators' theories

- The one behind it all is **Papa Legba**.
- The burglar might have been him, given the limp.
- He might be Ashdown in disguise, or Ashdown might be the one behind it all.

## Open questions

- Where is Leon? And Delroy?
- Who, or what, is *The Old Man's Tune* for?
- Who was Eddie S., and who paid for the horn?
- Who is "the Gentleman"? Who is "C."?
- Who paid the burglar £50?
- Whose deal is still open?
- What happens to Kieran if he plays the tune all the way through?
