# Dramatis Personae

Everyone the investigators have met so far, and what they know about each of them. Updated after each session.

## The Folly

**DCI Thomas Nightingale.** Head of the Folly and London's senior practitioner. Currently looking after the trumpet, the silver crown and the silver dollar from the Mysterioso. He has also received a formal letter of complaint from Dr Carmichael about PC O'Neil's conduct.

## The Blue Flame

### The Mysterioso

**Gerry Pike.** Manager of the Mysterioso, a basement jazz club off Bateman Street. Sweaty and jovial, and keen to avoid trouble with the police, the press or his licence.

**An elderly regular.** A woman in her eighties at a corner table, who looked as though she'd been sitting there for decades. She didn't give her name.

### The Leon Marsh Quintet

**Leon Marsh.** Trumpeter with a Tuesday residency at the Mysterioso. Vanished in a blue flame mid-set, in front of a full room. Missing.

**Kieran Doyle.** Second trumpet. Young, eager, and devoted to Leon. He took Leon's trumpet from the stage in the confusion and ran. PC O'Neil chased him but lost him in the crowd. He was later found at his bedsit in Kentish Town, playing the trumpet over and over. He hasn't slept since.

**Dee Halloran.** Double bass, and the band's organiser. Protective of the younger players. She didn't say much after PC O'Neil's approach.

**Len Zeiss.** The quintet's drummer. Arrested by PC O'Neil for not knowing where Kieran had gone, interviewed, and released without caution. Unlikely to be pleased to see the police again.

### Others

**Rupert Ashdown.** Polite, well-dressed, late twenties. Says he's "with the Cabinet Office, in a manner of speaking". He was very interested in where the trumpet had gone and not at all interested in Leon. He left a business card.

**Richard "Lord" Grant.** Veteran jazz trumpeter, back on the scene after a long time away. He looked at photographs of the trumpet and recognised some of the names scratched on it. He'd heard Leon play.

## The Bookshop

**Saffron Jackson.** Manager of Stonewaters bookshop.

**Warwick Anderson.** A frightened bookseller at Stonewaters.

**The Spirit of Books and Reading.** A lonely spirit living in the bookshop. After PC O'Neil read to it, it gave him a first edition of Langston Hughes's *The First Book of Jazz*, which he is now trying to sell online.
