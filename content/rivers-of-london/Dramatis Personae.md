# Dramatis Personae

Everyone the investigators have met so far, and what they know about each of them. Updated after each session.

## The Folly

**DCI Thomas Nightingale.** Head of the Folly and London's senior practitioner. He is looking after the trumpet, the silver crown and the silver dollar from the Mysterioso. He has also received a formal letter of complaint from Dr Carmichael about PC O'Neil's conduct. When the investigators asked for help, he examined the coins: he sensed that the warm one belongs to an open deal and the cold ones to closed deals. His opinion of Rupert Ashdown: a Cabinet Office flunky, and a coward. The team reported to him the morning after Kieran's disappearance. He has taken Dr Carmichael on for training and started him on Latin.

**Dr Abdul Haqq Walid.** The Folly's doctor. He told the team about an earlier case in the Folly's files, and sent Dr Carmichael to Nightingale for training.

## The Blue Flame

### The Mysterioso

**Gerry Pike.** Manager of the Mysterioso, a basement jazz club off Bateman Street. Sweaty and jovial, and keen to avoid trouble with the police, the press or his licence.

**Maud Fenwick.** The elderly regular at the corner table, who looked as though she'd been sitting there for decades. Delroy Fenwick's twin sister, older by twenty minutes. When the investigators met her again, she told them about Delroy's disappearance, "the Gentleman", the bill coming due, and the coin left behind.

### The Leon Marsh Quintet

**Leon Marsh.** Trumpeter with a Tuesday residency at the Mysterioso. Vanished in a blue flame mid-set, in front of a full room. Missing. His flat was being searched by a burglar when the investigators arrived.

**Kieran Doyle.** Second trumpet. Young, eager, and devoted to Leon. He took Leon's trumpet from the stage in the confusion and ran. PC O'Neil chased him but lost him in the crowd. He was later found at his bedsit in Kentish Town, playing the trumpet over and over. He then started playing an open mic, suddenly far better than he had any right to be. When the investigators tried to arrest him mid-tune, he disappeared, just as Leon had. Missing.

**Dee Halloran.** Double bass, and the band's organiser. Protective of the younger players. She rang PC O'Neil, worried about Kieran, and sent a photo of his set list.

**Len Zeiss.** The quintet's drummer. Arrested by PC O'Neil for not knowing where Kieran had gone, interviewed, and released without caution. Unlikely to be pleased to see the police again.

### Others

**Delroy Fenwick.** A London trumpeter whose name is scratched on the trumpet, and Maud's twin brother. He disappeared in the 1970s, leaving a silver crown behind. He had an arrangement with someone he called "the Gentleman".

**The burglar.** A local addict with a limp, caught searching Leon's flat. Someone paid him £50 to look for a trumpet. He dropped Leon's notebook while trying to get away.

**Rupert Ashdown.** Polite, well-dressed, late twenties. Says he's "with the Cabinet Office, in a manner of speaking". He was very interested in where the trumpet had gone and not at all interested in Leon. He left a business card signed "C.". In St James's Park he folded when Dr Boedscu threatened to ring his aunt. He says he only passes on what he's sent: documents from C. on cream paper, including a list of dangerous instruments, with instructions not to touch the trumpet or let it be played.

**The smiling man.** A man in a suit in the crowd at Kieran's open mic. As Kieran vanished, he caught the investigators' eye, shrugged, and was lost in the scrum.

**Richard "Lord" Grant.** Veteran jazz trumpeter, back on the scene after a long time away. He looked at photographs of the trumpet and recognised some of the names scratched on it. He'd heard Leon play.

**Dr Boedscu's aunt.** A Permanent Secretary at the Home Office. Her name was enough to make Ashdown talk. She has invited Julian to tea.

## The Bookshop

**Saffron Jackson.** Manager of Stonewaters bookshop.

**Warwick Anderson.** A frightened bookseller at Stonewaters.

**The Spirit of Books and Reading.** A lonely spirit living in the bookshop. After PC O'Neil read to it, it gave him a first edition of Langston Hughes's *The First Book of Jazz*. He has been trying to sell it online, and has now been invited to a pub in Smithfield to discuss a sale.
