# The Blue Flame — Case Summary

**Case:** The Blue Flame
**Sessions:** 3 November, 10 November and 17 November 2026
**Investigators:** PC Harry O'Neil · Dr Alex Carmichael · Dr Julian Boedscu
**Status:** Closed (unsolved)

> Two trumpeters gone in a blue flame, half a century apart, and one more vanishing in front of a full room. Each time, a silver coin was left behind.

For the full clue list, see [[./Blues Clues|Blues Clues]]. For everyone involved, see [[./Dramatis Personae|Dramatis Personae]].

## How it began

PC O'Neil's first edition of *The First Book of Jazz*, a gift from the spirit at Stonewaters bookshop, led the team to a basement jazz club off Bateman Street, the **Mysterioso**. Tucked inside the book was a photograph of a band at **the Blue Lantern, Gerrard Street**, in Coronation week, May 1937. The trumpeter's horn had a distinctive dent in the bell.

## Night one: the Mysterioso

- **Leon Marsh** was playing his Tuesday residency. Between numbers he kept watching the stair door. He refused to play his usual closer, *The Old Man's Tune*, and counted in something else.
- Every flame in the room leaned towards the stage. Leon stood calmly inside a **silent blue fire** that gave off no heat. When it went out, he was gone.
- **Left behind:**
  - a cold silver crown dated 1937
  - a warm silver dollar dated 1921, on a chain with the clasp still fastened
  - the set list, with *The Old Man's Tune* crossed out hard and "NOT ANY MORE" in the margin
- **Kieran Doyle**, Leon's young second trumpet, took Leon's trumpet in the confusion and ran. The team tracked him to a bedsit in Kentish Town, where he was playing a fragment of a tune over and over, saying the trumpet wanted it "finished".
- **The trumpet:** old, American, with the same dented bell as the 1937 photograph. Names are scratched round the bell. The newest is Leon's; the oldest that can still be read is **Eddie S.**
- **Len Zeiss**, the drummer, was arrested by PC O'Neil and released without caution. Dr Carmichael wrote to Nightingale complaining about PC O'Neil's conduct.
- **Lord Grant** identified several names from photographs of the trumpet, including Delroy Fenwick and Eddie S.
- **Handed over:** the trumpet, crown and dollar went to Nightingale. Nobody has touched the trumpet with bare hands since.

## Night two: Leon's trail

- **Leon's flat:** a burglar with a limp was searching it. He'd been paid £50 to find a trumpet. The team caught him, and he dropped **Leon's notebook**.
- **The notebook** described a polite man who appeared at the bar after Leon played the tune alone after closing. There were "terms". It was "a price, not a gift". A "best year" followed, then "it would be collected". Leon stopped playing the tune, and his last entry warned: *"DON'T let him play it."*
- **Maud Fenwick**, Delroy Fenwick's twin sister, said Delroy was ordinary for twenty years, then suddenly brilliant. He spoke of "the Gentleman" and said "the bill always comes due". When he vanished in the 1970s, a silver crown was left behind.
- **Nightingale** read the coins: a **warm** coin means a deal still open; a **cold** one, a deal closed.
- **Rupert Ashdown**, "with the Cabinet Office, in a manner of speaking", wanted to know where the trumpet was. His card is signed only *"With compliments. — C."*
- **The night ended** with Dee Halloran ringing to say Kieran was playing an open mic, and was suspiciously good. His set list ended with *The Old Man's Tune ?*

## Night three: the open mic

- **Ashdown in St James's Park.** At first he was polished and gave nothing away. He folded when Dr Boedscu threatened to ring his aunt, a Permanent Secretary at the Home Office. Ashdown says he knows very little. Documents reach him from "C." on cream paper, including a **list of dangerous instruments** with clear instructions: don't touch the trumpet, and don't let it be played.
- **The open mic.** The team went to Kieran's gig. As he played the old tune, they tried to arrest him to stop him playing. He disappeared, just as Leon had.
- **The smiling man.** In the crowd, a smiling man in a suit caught their eye, shrugged, and was lost in the scrum.
- **Statements.** PC O'Neil took statements from the witnesses.

#### Aftermath

- **Reporting in.** The next morning, the team reported to Nightingale.
- **Dr Walid** told them about an earlier case in the Folly's files. He has since sent Dr Carmichael to Nightingale for training, and Nightingale has started him on Latin.
- **Harry's book sale.** PC O'Neil has had a text inviting him to a pub in Smithfield to discuss selling his copy of *The First Book of Jazz*.
- **Julian's invitation.** Dr Boedscu has been invited to tea with his aunt.

## The pattern

Ordinary musicians suddenly become very good, apparently after an arrangement with a polite man. The tune is part of it. The bill comes due, the musician vanishes in blue flame, and a silver crown is left behind. It has happened at least since the 1970s, and the trumpet has been in London since 1937.

## The investigators' theories

- **Papa Legba** is behind it all.
- **Ashdown** may be involved, in disguise or otherwise.

None of this is confirmed.

## Still open

- Where are Leon, Kieran and Delroy?
- Who was Eddie S., and who paid for the horn ("Horn's paid for. Not by me.")?
- Who is "the Gentleman"? Who is the smiling man? Who is "C."?
- Who else has learned *The Old Man's Tune*?
- Whose deal is still open?
